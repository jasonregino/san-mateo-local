Where to Watch the Sunset in San Mateo

Updated September 30, 2026

San Mateo faces the bay, so you won't see the sun drop into the ocean here. Here's where to watch it go down anyway, which spots stay open late enough, and which season each one works in.

One thing first, because it trips people up. San Mateo faces the bay, and the bay is to the east, so there's no sun sinking into the ocean here. What you get instead is better than it sounds. From high ground you watch the sun drop behind the hills to the west, and from the bayfront the whole sky and the water turn pink and gold. The catch is closing times. A few of the best spots lock up right around sunset, and when that happens depends on the month. Here's where to go, and when each one actually works.

Five places to watch the sun go down in San Mateo, each checked against its closing time so you don't show up to a locked gate.

The local pick, fall and winter

Seal Point Park

Out where East 3rd Avenue meets the bay · Closes 7 PM

The one we'd send a friend to first. It's the old San Mateo dump, capped and turned into a grassy hill with views all the way around the bay. Walk up the mound and watch the hills go dark while the water lights up. The park closes at 7 PM, so this is a fall and winter spot: from late September until the clocks spring forward in March, the sun is down before closing. In summer it sets after the gate. The dog park on site closes at sunset.

For summer evenings

Sugarloaf Mountain, from Laurelwood Park

Hilltop · Trails start at Laurelwood Park

The high ground in San Mateo. Dirt trails climb from Laurelwood Park to viewpoints on Sugarloaf, about 1.7 miles round trip with roughly 470 feet of climbing on the most popular route. Look west and the sun goes down behind the ridge, then turn around for the whole bay glowing below. Laurelwood is listed as open until 10 PM, which makes this the spot for long summer evenings when the bayfront parks have already closed. Bring a light for the walk down, it gets dark fast under the trees.

Along the bay

Ryder Park

Bayfront · Next door to Seal Point

Right next to Seal Point on the shoreline, with a boardwalk out over the marsh. Its listed hours run to 8 PM, an hour later than Seal Point, so it covers most of the year except the longest days, roughly early May through mid-August.

San Francisco Bay Trail

Shoreline path · Coyote Point to the San Mateo Bridge

Walk or bike the shoreline at golden hour and watch the hills to the west turn gold while the bay goes pink. Seal Point and Ryder Park are both easy places to hop on.

Check the clock before Coyote Point

Coyote Point Recreation Area

County park · Closing time changes through the year

It shows up on every sunset list, but read this first. The county closes the park on a schedule that shifts through the year, and for most of it the gate shuts before the sun goes down. It works in April and in November and December. The rest of the year, head down the shoreline to Seal Point or Ryder Park instead.
